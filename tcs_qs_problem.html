💡 Motivation

Why This Problem
Matters

Nitrogen‑vacancy (NV) centres in diamond are versatile quantum sensors capable of measuring multiple physical properties (including magnetic and electric fields, temperature, stress, strain, humidity, etc.) with high sensitivity and robustness, under ambient conditions.

Translating their in-lab demonstrations into deployable sensing systems requires systematic optimization of experimental control parameters while accounting for realistic imperfections and inherent noise mechanisms.

Impact Statement: Even small improvements in the sensor's performance parameters such as magnetic field sensitivity, bandwidth, or dynamic range can significantly improve the suitability of NV-based magnetometers for industrial applications.

🎯 Problem Statement

Detailed Problem
Description

Considering various tuneable input parameters such as laser power, microwave power, modulation depth and technical noise sources (including laser and microwave broadening, detection noise, etc.) in continuous-wave (CW) optically detected magnetic resonance (ODMR) scheme and NV diamond magnetometry model, employ a data-driven closed-loop optimization pipeline that tunes experimental control parameters to improve sensor performance in the presence of non-ideal experimental conditions.
🔑 Key Requirements
  • Inputs
    A list of considered input parameters for characterising or modelling NV diamond magnetometry experimentation.
  • Objective
    Optimise the performance metrics achieved at the output employing AI-based approaches over normal control methods.
  • Constraint
    Account for experimental nonidealities and technical noises.
